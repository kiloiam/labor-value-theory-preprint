Dear Editor,

I am submitting the manuscript "The Labor Theory of Value as a Historical Category:
The Contemporary Crisis of Value" for consideration in your journal.

Grounding its analysis in historical materialism, the article argues that the labor
theory of value is a historically specific category premised on wage labor, socially
necessary labor time, and generalized commodity exchange, rather than a universal
economic principle. By constructing a revised value model that incorporates automated
technological capital, it demonstrates that as automated systems become capable in
principle of performing productive functions independently of living labor, variable
capital is systematically expelled and the production of surplus value tends toward
exhaustion. Profit is thereby transmuted into a negative political cost of social
stabilization under private property relations. The article further elaborates the
"four-fold impossibility" of commodity society's self-repair and proposes a theoretical
criterion for the historical reconfiguration of production relations in a
post-value-category era.

The manuscript is original, unpublished, and not under consideration elsewhere. All
authors have approved the submission. A preprint version has been released openly
under CC BY 4.0 and is citable as:
