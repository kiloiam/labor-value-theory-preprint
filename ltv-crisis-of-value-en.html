What the preprint argues

The "contemporary crisis of value" in the title is not a recession, a financial crisis or a cyclical fall in the profit rate. It denotes a structural crisis of the value-category itself: value as a form of social relation loses the conditions that make it operative.

The paper's chain runs: automated systems in principle detach from living labor → variable capital is systematically and irreversibly expelled → surplus-value production tends toward exhaustion → the value-category and the commodity-form depending on it break down → profit becomes a "negative political cost of social stabilization" (P) maintaining the shell of private property.

The paper then elaborates the "four-fold impossibility" of commodity society's self-repair, arguing for an irreversible internal rupture in the value-structure. This is what separates crisis from cycle: cycles can be repaired, whereas the paper holds that this rupture cannot be repaired by commodity society itself.

Its conclusion accordingly reads the obsolescence of the LTV as a sign that commodity society has reached its historical endpoint, and identifies the central issue of a post-value-category era as the historical reconfiguration of production relations: liberating automated productive capacity from private appropriation and reorienting it toward the socialized organization of use-value. This is a normative theoretical criterion, not a forecast or a policy program.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a theory of economic crises?
Not in the usual sense. It concerns the structural breakdown of the value-category as a social form, not movements in output, employment or financial indicators.
What exactly is the "four-fold impossibility"?
The paper's term for four lines of argument that commodity society cannot repair the rupture in the value-structure. Consult the paper for the items and reasoning; this guide does not reconstruct them.
Can the rupture be empirically tested?
The paper offers theoretical analysis and a structural claim of irreversibility. It is a non-peer-reviewed preprint, and this guide does not present it as empirically confirmed.
