What the preprint argues

In Marxian usage this distinction is neither a moral judgment nor a useful/useless split. It asks whether a labor stands within the capital-valorization relation and directly produces surplus value. The same kind of work can fall on different sides under different relations of production.

Recurring disputes concern transport and storage, commercial and circulation labor, the treatment of management and supervision, and the status of service and public-sector work. Different interpretive traditions answer differently. This page does not adjudicate; it notes that 'productive labor' is a relational category, not a list of industries.

The preprint bears on this because it discusses a structural consequence of variable capital being systematically expelled by automated systems: the exhaustion of the surplus-value production mechanism. It offers no industry classification table and no classificatory scholarship; it is a non-peer-reviewed online preprint.

Frequently asked questions

Does unproductive mean unimportant?
No. The distinction describes a labor's position relative to capital valorization, not its social importance or skill level.
Is all service labor unproductive?
Not uniformly; it depends on the valorization relation and on the interpretive tradition. A separate page on this site discusses the service sector.
Does the preprint propose a new classification?
No. It examines the overall tendency of the surplus-value mechanism once variable capital is expelled; it is not a taxonomic revision.
