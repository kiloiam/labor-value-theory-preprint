What the preprint argues

Digital labour research usually asks whether paid and unpaid platform activity — content production, data labeling, clicks, contributions of training data — counts as productive labor, creates value, and how it is appropriated. Those questions stay inside the LTV framework and presuppose that wage labor and generalized commodity exchange still hold.

This paper does not discuss digital labour and offers no valuation of user-generated content or data. Read through its framework, platform and data labor would be cases where the historical premises still hold: however the form of labor changes, as long as it remains measurable by socially necessary labor time and socially validated through commodity exchange, the value-category still has an object.

Applying the paper's "breakdown of the value-category" directly to today's platform economy would therefore overreach. The paper confines its argument to the limit case in which automated systems can in principle perform production independently of living labor, and stresses that this is a structural tendency.

For specialist literature on digital labour, platform capitalism or data valuation, this page only marks the boundary.

Frequently asked questions

Does the paper study platform labour or data value?
No. Its scenario is automated production and a revised value model; it contains no analysis of digital labour.
Is it useful for digital labour research at all?
At most as a distinction of levels: digital labour asks how labor forms value; this paper asks whether the value-category's premises still hold. They are different questions.
Can it explain the appropriation of AI training data?
Not advised. That requires a concrete analysis of labor processes and appropriation mechanisms which the paper does not provide.
