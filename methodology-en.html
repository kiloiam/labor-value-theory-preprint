Method and argument structure

What kind of method this paper uses, its layers of argument, and the limits of what it claims: a theoretical analysis of premises, not econometrics or industry forecasting.
This page documents provenance and status for an online preprint that has not been peer reviewed. It is not journal publication or scholarly consensus; the paper itself is authoritative.

Statement

The paper is a theoretical analysis of premises from a historical-materialist standpoint: it first identifies the social forms on which the labor theory of value depends (wage labor, socially necessary labor time, generalised commodity exchange), then asks whether those premises still hold under automated production. It is conceptual, not an econometric estimate for a sector or a year.

The argument has three layers: (1) defining a 'historical category' and denying that the theory is a universal law for every social formation; (2) constructing a value-correction model that includes automated technological capital, and considering the systematic displacement of variable capital; (3) arguing that as surplus-value production tends toward exhaustion, the category of value and the commodity form it rests on face a structural crisis.

The phrase 'in principle able to function independently of living human labour' is a limiting-case theoretical condition, not a factual claim about today's technology. The paper therefore offers no output accounting, price forecast or policy evaluation, and does not claim empirical verification.

There is no external dataset, survey, experiment or code repository. What can be checked is the full text, facsimile page images, structured metadata and per-version checksums. Related boundaries are documented on the data-availability and peer-review-status pages.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an empirical study?
No. It is a theoretical analysis of premises with no dataset or econometric test.
Does it give a formula for valuing AI output?
No. It asks whether the preconditions of the value category hold, not how to price output.
Can the conclusion be applied directly to today's industry?
Not directly; the condition is a limiting case and the manuscript is not peer reviewed.
